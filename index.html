Why

Every exchange runs its own order book, and traders usually keep them within a fraction of a percent. Kraken's CAD books are small, so a wave of sells moved them a long way. Blockstream's Adam Back pointed to a likely market-maker malfunction: Kraken's own USDC/CAD rate drifted far from the real one, around 1.43. Kraken hasn't confirmed a cause.

It stayed open because almost nobody could reach it. You needed Canadian dollars already sitting on Kraken. Fat fingers, liquidation cascades, stablecoin wobbles and outages open the same kind of gap on other exchanges. The people who catch them are the ones already watching.

04 · Questions

Before you trust a number on this page.

What is this?

A live price dashboard. It compares what BTC, ETH and SOL cost right now on Kraken, Coinbase, Binance, Bitstamp and OKX, plus Upbit in Korea and bitFlyer in Japan, and flags when one exchange drifts away from the rest. It shows information. It doesn't trade, hold funds or tell you what to do.

What actually happened on Kraken?

From about 20:00 UTC on November 20 to 15:45 UTC on November 21, Kraken's Canadian-dollar markets traded far below every other exchange. SOL printed C$32.00 while the rest of the market was near C$95.86, and BTC printed C$24,250 against about C$82,687. Kraken said it found no breach and that executed trades stand. It hasn't published a confirmed cause.

Could anyone have bought SOL for C$32?

Mostly no. You needed a Kraken account with Canadian dollars already on it, and new CAD only reaches Kraken by bank transfer from a Canadian account. That's a big part of why the gap stayed open for hours. The numbers on this page come from Kraken's public 15-minute price data. They're not a record of anyone's fills.

How often do gaps like this happen?

Rarely. Most of the time the big global exchanges agree within about 0.1%, which is less than trading fees. Bigger gaps come from things like thin local-currency markets, fat-finger orders, liquidation cascades, stablecoin wobbles and exchange outages. We can't predict when the next one will happen, or whether it will.

Where do the prices come from?

Straight from each exchange's public market-data feed to your browser, over WebSocket connections your browser opens itself. Prices don't pass through a server of ours. If an exchange's feed lags, drops or sends a bad price, the dashboard shows exactly that.

How is the "market price" worked out?

It's the median of the live USD, USDT and USDC prices across Kraken, Coinbase, Binance, Bitstamp and OKX. USDT prices are adjusted by the live USDT/USD rate. CAD and EUR prices are converted at the rate their own markets imply, which is checked against a daily bank rate and replaced by it if the two disagree by more than 2.5%.

Why does Kraken CAD usually sit a little off the others?

Currency conversion. Turning a CAD price into US dollars adds a small error, usually under 0.2%. That's normal. A real misprice is many times bigger, and that's what the alert line is for.

What's the Korea premium?

Crypto in Korea often trades a few percent above the rest of the world, which is called the "kimchi premium". Japan sometimes drifts too. These are converted at the official bank exchange rate, so the dashboard shows the real local premium. Capital controls and local-account rules mean most people can't trade across that gap, so Korea and Japan are shown separately and don't trigger the main gap alert.

Is a gap free money?

No. To use a gap you need funds already on both exchanges, an account that can trade those markets, and enough depth in the order book at that price. Fees, withdrawal times, limits and slippage all eat into it, and gaps can close before you act. What the dashboard shows is a price, not a trade you're guaranteed to get.

Do I need an account, a wallet or API keys?

No. There's no sign-up, no wallet connection and no keys. The dashboard only reads public prices. It can't see or touch your exchange accounts.

How do alerts work?

Browser alerts are live. Allow notifications and keep the tab open: when any book crosses your line you get a notification, a sound and an entry in the gap log. Alerts run from the open page, so they stop when you close the tab or your device sleeps. Email and text alerts aren't built yet.

Do you store my data?

Your alert line and gap log are saved in your own browser and never sent to us. If you tap "I want email alerts" or "I want text alerts", we record that someone asked for that channel, nothing more. We don't collect email addresses or phone numbers. See the policy.

Is this financial advice?

No. Nothing here is advice or a recommendation to buy, sell or hold anything. Any action you take based on what you see here is your own decision and your own risk.
